What it actually is

A tub warmed by fire, not a plug

A wood-fired hot tub heats its water with a wood-burning stove. This one is internally loaded, the burner sits inside the tub, so the heat passes straight into the water and nothing takes up room on your patio.

The result is a slower, quieter kind of heat. It takes a while to come up to temperature, and that waiting is part of the ritual for most people who choose one. You light it, it warms, you soak.

The other layout

Or a burner that stands alongside

An externally loaded tub keeps the stove outside the water, beside the tub. It frees up every inch of the interior and makes loading the fire a little easier to reach.

Which one suits you comes down to how you use the space and how much room you want inside. We talk it through on every enquiry rather than leaving you to guess.

The honest comparison

Wood-fired against electric

An electric spa is convenient. Set a temperature, walk away, come back to warm water, and pay for it on the standing electricity bill.

A wood-fired tub asks a little more of you and gives something different back: a real flame, a look that ages into a garden rather than out of it, and no daily draw on the mains. If you want the best of both, our tubs can take an electric or hybrid heater as an option, so you keep the fire and gain the convenience. Neither path is wrong. The question is which one suits how you actually live.

Electric spa

Electric is set and forget. Wood-fired is a thing you do, and that is rather the point.
